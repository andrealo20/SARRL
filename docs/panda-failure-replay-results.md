# Recovered Panda failure timing

All twenty unsafe filtered episodes from the v2.6 joint-filter study reproduce
with the unchanged production source. Every retained episode field agrees
under the original numerical tolerance. The [recovery protocol](panda-failure-replay-methods.md)
was frozen at `f29dd979d3ef66d894a918935d252cb606dd73e7`; production remains
`6891dd27edf8b28514160d1ade350e3213826058`.

The [public evidence](../results/panda_failure_replay_v26) adds full traces for
ten clean and ten noisy fast moves at 40 ms delay. These are the twenty
already used unsafe cases selected from all 480 original records. The recovery
adds 30,000 retained physical integration calls on used seeds, with no new
candidate or confirmation tasks. Original records and acceptance decisions
are unchanged.

## First violations occur under commands sent before abort

Every first measured violation occurs after the filter latches braking,
during an interval still executing a command sent before abort. None first
occurs in an interval generated by a braking command. First violations occur
at 0.124 to 0.154 s; aborts occur at 0.09 to 0.12 s and braking first reaches
the plant at 0.13 to 0.16 s.

| Fast-move condition | Reproduced cases | First violation strictly before braking arrival | At preceding interval's right boundary | Cases with later violations under braking |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Clean, 40 ms | 10 | 8 | 2 | 7 |
| Noisy, 40 ms | 10 | 5 | 5 | 7 |
| Total | 20 | 13 | 7 | 14 |

The seven boundary samples have the same timestamp as braking arrival, up to
roundoff. They are the final integration samples of the preceding command
interval. Classification follows that interval and the recorded FIFO command
identity, rather than comparing rounded timestamps. All twenty first
violations therefore belong to commands sent before abort, including those
seven boundary samples.

All measured violations are velocity violations. No position violation or
contact occurs. The largest velocity-limit excess is 2.664647 rad/s. Across
the twenty traces, 358 physical substeps violate a measured limit:

| Integration phase | Clean substeps | Noisy substeps | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before abort | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| After abort, previous commands still in flight | 40 | 32 | 72 |
| Braking command delivered | 143 | 143 | 286 |

Fourteen cases continue violating after braking arrives. This count describes
measured states during braking intervals; it does not attribute those states
to braking or measure the effect of a different fallback.

## Reproduction and interpretation

The audit checks all twenty episode records, 6,000 causal controller cycles,
30,000 saved physical substeps and 206 independently checked feasible QP
projections. It reconstructs controller, estimator, measurement noise, FIFO
delivery and physical violation maxima with zero new physical integration
calls. The public audit needs no local resumable cell shards.

The manifest retains the original campaign's runtime metadata, including its
four-worker count, under `runtime`. Its top-level `workers: 1` records this
serial recovery. Numerical libraries and model provenance match the original
campaign.

These traces establish event order and command provenance for every retained
unsafe filtered case. They do not isolate a unique cause, validate a narrower
delay set or show that another braking law would prevent the violations.
Reactive braking cannot replace a command already executing at the plant.
The next Panda design must address the admissibility of commands while they
are in flight, including the states from which delayed braking remains
effective. That design requires a separate frozen protocol and unused
confirmation seeds. The cumulative bank remains the reference and the v2.6
filter candidate still fails its original screen.
